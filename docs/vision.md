# World Sim: Design Statement

## The idea

World Sim is an AI-driven simulation of real life on Earth that you play by voice. You can go anywhere and do anything, and the world keeps up. Nothing tells you what to do.

The world is real. It has real cities, real stores with real hours, and people with lives of their own. It keeps moving when you aren't looking, and it remembers everything you do.

There is no objective and no ending; the story is whatever happens while you live there. It isn't a game about the world. It's the world, with you in it.

Voice is the whole interface. You can start a session from the car without touching your phone, or play at a desk. There are no graphics, on purpose: the picture is in your head.

## Where it comes from

It grew out of a theater-of-the-mind game: one voice describes a secret base, the other narrates breaking in. The rule that made it work was honesty. Every defense had a real weakness, and nobody could invent a new wall on the spot to win.

World Sim keeps the honesty and removes the walls. There's no base, no mission, and no edge of the map. It's the whole Earth, played fairly.

## The feel

It should feel like living inside a book. A good novel never shows you a picture, yet you know what the room smells like. Your imagination builds the world, so it stays yours.

It should also be a game you never notice is a game. Nothing announces itself as a mechanic, a quest, or a twist. Things just happen, the way they do in life.

The surface is simple: a voice and your own words. Underneath sits a world that has worked out far more than it ever says.

The signature moment is thinking "it really thought of that," then realizing nobody planned it. A Korean hospital's non-emergency wing is closed because it's Sunday. Your girlfriend called the police because you went silent for three weeks.

## The core principle: the world is true

Everything rests on one idea: the world decides what's true, and each person perceives only their slice of it.

When something comes up for the first time, the world settles it. Reality decides first, and where reality is silent, the most plausible answer fills the gap. Once settled, it stays settled.

Then every mind gets only its own view. You notice what your character would notice. The people around you know only what they've seen or been told.

The narrator never asks what would be interesting. It asks what is true here, and what this person would actually notice. The details follow on their own:

- The bartender is still there next week, because he became real the first time you saw him.
- A tired mechanic hears the engine knock but misses the man following him.
- People lie, gossip, and track you through the traces you leave, because nobody gets the whole truth.
- Three months away really is three months: rent came due, calls went unanswered, and people acted on what they knew.

That's why this isn't a pile of features. Every detail is a consequence of the world being true.

## Pillars

Every design decision answers to these.

- **Freedom with weight.** You can try anything, and everything costs something. There's no undo or reload, and the dice are real. When a character dies, they stay dead, and you begin again as someone new.
- **Situations, not quests.** There are no objectives, markers, or checklists. Every life starts with its own friction: a job, a debt, a relationship, a problem. What you want grows out of that, and you decide what's worth chasing.
- **The world goes on.** Time moves, and people live their own lives whether or not you're watching. Ignore someone for months and they act on it. Even death doesn't reset it: your next life might walk past the store your last one burned.
- **You are a person, not an avatar.** Your character has a history, skills, a body, and people who know them. Who they are shapes what you notice and how things go.
- **Imagination is the screen.** The narration says what matters, not everything. Sound sets the mood without drawing the picture: the hum of a casino floor, wind along a desert fence. Images would overwrite what your mind builds.
- **The world plays fair.** Nothing appears from nowhere to punish you or save you. Every surprise traces back to something true, so it makes sense in hindsight.
- **Your choices are yours.** The world narrates what happens around you. It never decides what you do, think, or feel.

The one exception is sandbox mode: pure play in a separate copy of the world, so the real one stays honest.

## What it is not

The fastest way to ruin this is to make it feel like a game.

- **Not GTA.** A playground where nothing remembers you is a toy, not a world.
- **Not a quest log.** No markers, missions, XP bars, or "objective complete."
- **Not a pile of features.** If a detail needs its own system, the foundation is wrong.
- **Not contrived.** No drama manufactured to entertain you, no world out to get you, no plot armor.
- **Not a yes-man.** The world doesn't bend to please you. A plan works because it exploits something real, not because you said it with confidence.
- **Not a narrator who describes everything.** Every sentence should earn its place.
- **Not screen-first.** Images and menus pull you out of the world you're imagining.
- **Not slow.** Dead air while the world thinks breaks the spell.
- **Not a gimmick.** Five minutes of "I can do anything" isn't the goal. Coming back a hundred hours later is.

## How we'll know it's working

It works when you forget you're playing.

- You tell people what happened to you, not what the game did.
- Surprises make sense in hindsight: of course she called the police.
- You hesitate before a big choice, because it matters.
- You wonder what's happening back home while you're away.
- You return to a place and it's the same place.
- A short drive turns into a story you remember.
- When a character dies, it stings, and you're curious who you'll be next.
